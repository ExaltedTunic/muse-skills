# Recruiter question templates

Templates for the permit question in the Norway job search flow. Replace the bracketed parts, keep the framing.

## Rules that govern every template

1. Never use the word "sponsor" or "sponsorship" with a Norwegian employer. It reads as cost and legal exposure. Describe the actual ask: the employer confirms the job offer in UDI's system, and the candidate handles the application.
2. Describe the candidate's situation honestly from the private profile's verified residence facts. The templates in this file are the abroad-applicant branch: use their "applying from outside Norway, not yet resident" wording only when the profile records that the candidate is applying from abroad and is not resident in Norway. If the profile records lawful residence in Norway, use resident wording based on that verified fact and never use the abroad text. Never claim a home is set up unless a lease or purchase is signed.
3. Default timing: the permit question goes with the application, not before it. Ask first only when applying is expensive, such as an assessment, a case task, or a very long form.
4. Nothing goes out without the candidate's explicit sign-off on that specific message.
5. Only a written yes or no moves the permit-support status in the ledger. "We will look into it" stays at asked. Record who replied, when, and the key sentence.

## Standard permit question (abroad-applicant branch)

For a candidate whose profile verifies the abroad facts in this template, use it as written, changing only the company name:

> I am applying from outside Norway, so I would need a skilled-worker residence permit. Your part would be confirming the job offer in UDI's system, and I handle the application myself. Is that something [Company] can do for this role?

**Resident branch:** if the profile records lawful residence in Norway, do not use the abroad wording above. Generate the residence and authorization wording from the verified profile facts for that resident route, while keeping the permit mechanics that still apply.

Useful follow-up once they say yes:

> Have you confirmed an offer in UDI's system for a hire from outside the EEA before, and is there anything you would need from me at offer stage?

Do not ask about their internal timeline in the first message. It can wait for the reply.

## Staffing-authorised (`godkjent`) employers and consultancies placing at clients

Add this line to the standard question:

> I understand UDI also asks for a list of planned assignments confirmed by the client for staffing-authorised employers. Would a client assignment be confirmed up front in my case?

Permits through staffing-authorised (`godkjent`) employers can run one year at a time. That belongs in the ledger entry, not in the outreach.

## Employers of record

Default position: permit-support status is unlikely. Employers of record normally employ people who already hold Norwegian work authorization. Do not spend a tailored application here unless the candidate says otherwise.

If the candidate does want to ask, keep it to one factual question, with no permit pitch:

> For a hire based in Norway, would [Provider] be the employer on a Norwegian skilled-worker permit application, or do you require existing Norwegian work authorization before hiring?

A written yes changes the status. Anything else does not.

## Remote role, employer entity unclear

Where a posting says remote in Norway but does not name the hiring entity, the entity question comes before the permit question, because the permit answer depends on it. Both can go in the same application message. The wording below is the abroad-applicant branch; for a lawfully resident candidate, replace its residence sentence with the profile's resident wording and keep only the entity and permit mechanics that apply:

> One practical question: for this role, would I be employed by a Norwegian-registered entity, or through an employer of record? I am applying from outside Norway, so I would need a skilled-worker residence permit. Your part would be confirming the job offer in UDI's system, and I handle the application myself. Is that something you can do for this role?

If the answer is a foreign entity with no Norwegian registration and no employer of record willing to act as the employer, that is a blocker under the flow's objective. Log it and park the role.

## What not to write

- "Will you sponsor my visa?" Wrong word, wrong framing, and this route is a residence permit, not a visa.
- "I am resident in Norway." Use only when the candidate profile verifies lawful residence in Norway; in the abroad-applicant branch, it is not true.
- "I can start as soon as I have an offer." The candidate cannot start until the permit is granted, unless the police have issued a confirmation of early employment start (tidlig arbeidsstart) for this employer; never promise a start date that ignores permit processing or the conditions of such a confirmation.
- Any promise of a start date that ignores permit processing time.
- "We have a home set up." Unless a lease or purchase is actually signed, say the relocation plans are established.
- Passport numbers, ID numbers, or bank details at any point before an offer. Asking for them early is also a scam signal in the other direction.

## Handling replies

- Yes, in writing, from someone who can commit (recruiter, hiring manager, HR): permit-support status becomes confirmed. The ledger gets the person, date, channel, and the key sentence.
- No, in writing: permit-support status becomes declined, the role moves to Parked, with the same ledger detail.
- No reply after 7 days: draft one follow-up for the candidate. The candidate decides whether it goes out.
- No reply 7 days after that: flag it for the candidate. Do not send a third message without approval.
- A dodge or "we will look into it": stays at asked, with the reply quoted in the ledger history.
